Teaching Philosophy

I approach biology education as an opportunity to help students understand the living world as a collection of interconnected, dynamic systems. Rather than emphasizing the memorization of isolated facts, I encourage students to investigate how biological systems work, how they persist, and how they change across time and levels of organization.

My teaching is grounded in evolutionary thinking, scientific inquiry, and evidence-based reasoning. I emphasize the relationships among biological structures, processes, and patterns, helping students build explanations that connect molecular and cellular mechanisms to organisms, populations, and evolutionary change.

I believe meaningful scientific learning requires opportunities to question, predict, observe, analyze, and revise one's understanding. Through laboratory investigations, collaborative problem-solving, computational models, visual representations, and authentic scientific data, I aim to make abstract concepts tangible and encourage students to participate actively in scientific discovery.

I recognize that students enter science classrooms with different experiences, strengths, and needs. My goal is to maintain meaningful scientific expectations while providing multiple pathways for students to engage with concepts, develop confidence, and demonstrate their understanding.

Ultimately, I want students to leave my courses not only with a stronger understanding of biology, but with the ability to think scientifically, evaluate evidence, recognize connections, and approach unfamiliar questions with curiosity.

Courses & Curriculum

I currently teach BIO 112 at Des Moines Area Community College (DMACC). I organize the course around a central question: How does life work, persist, and change?

Across the course, students connect foundational biological ideas to cells and energy, continuity and inheritance, molecular biology, and evolution. Laboratory investigations, model-building, authentic data, and practice explaining evidence help students connect processes across levels of biological organization.

As I develop additional courses and learning experiences, I aim to maintain clear objectives, meaningful scientific expectations, opportunities for practice and feedback, and assessments aligned with what students should be able to explain and apply.

Current assignments, assessments, grades, and course-specific announcements for enrolled students remain available through DMACC's official learning systems.
