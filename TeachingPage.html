About My Teaching

I am an Instructor of Biology at Des Moines Area Community College. I believe students learn biology best when they can connect ideas, examine evidence, and explain how living systems work, persist, and change. Evolutionary thinking provides a unifying framework for understanding both the diversity of life and the processes that connect organisms across scales.

My courses emphasize conceptual understanding over memorization. Students practice making predictions, investigating questions, interpreting authentic data, and revising explanations in response to evidence. Laboratory investigations, comparative examples, visual models, collaborative problem-solving, and active-learning activities help them apply biological ideas rather than simply recognize terminology.

I strive to create inclusive learning environments that recognize different backgrounds, experiences, and ways of engaging with science. I use varied opportunities for practice, feedback, and demonstration of understanding while maintaining clear learning objectives and meaningful scientific expectations. I also explore how scientific computing, computational models, and digital anatomy can make complex systems more visible, testable, and accessible.

Teaching Specialties and Interests

  • Introductory Biology (majors and non-majors)
  • Organismal Biology, Zoology, and Herpetology
  • Vertebrate Anatomy and Comparative Morphology
  • Ecology- and evolution-centered biology instruction
  • Laboratory inquiry, active learning, and authentic data analysis
  • Scientific computing, computational models, and digital anatomy tools
