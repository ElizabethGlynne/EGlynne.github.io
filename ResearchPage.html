About My Research

I am a conceptual evolutionary biologist interested in understanding the evolutionary and ecological processes that shape phenotypic diversity. I earned my Ph.D. in Ecology and Evolutionary Biology at Iowa State University, where I worked with Dr. Dean C. Adams. My research examines how changes in body size, sexual dimorphism, and morphological integration contribute to broader patterns of morphological evolution, particularly among vertebrates.

My doctoral research investigated the evolutionary consequences of miniaturization and variation in sexual size dimorphism. Using geometric morphometrics, phylogenetic comparative methods, and quantitative analyses of morphological variation, I examine how evolutionary patterns emerge across species and how relationships among morphological traits may change through evolutionary time.

Beyond these research questions, I am interested in the intersection of evolutionary biology, computational methods, and digital anatomy. Three-dimensional imaging, CT scanning, photogrammetry, and accessible computational tools offer opportunities to investigate biological form while expanding how students and researchers interact with anatomical and evolutionary data.

My broader goal is to connect rigorous evolutionary research with scientific education. I am committed to making biological concepts, quantitative approaches, and research methodologies more accessible through open-source tools, interactive learning experiences, and inclusive science education.
